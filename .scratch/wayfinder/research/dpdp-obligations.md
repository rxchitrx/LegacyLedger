# Research: DPDP obligations — retention, erasure, lawful exemptions

Scope: what India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the DPDP Rules, 2025 require or permit on (a) retention, (b) correction/erasure rights and fiduciary duties, (c) exemptions for legal obligations and legal claims — verified against official texts as of 18 Oct 2026, when the substantive provisions are not yet in force (phased to ~13 May 2027).

## Findings

- **Phased commencement — most duties are not yet live.** Under commencement notification G.S.R. 843(E) (13 Nov 2025), only institutional provisions are in force today; the Act's substantive sections — s.8 (fiduciary obligations, incl. erasure), ss.11–14 (rights), s.17 (exemptions) — commence 18 months after publication, computed as **~13 May 2027** (13–15 May uncertainty; source notes it as interpretation until officially confirmed). Rules 1, 2, 17–21 are in force since 13 Nov 2025; Rule 4 (Consent Managers) on 13 Nov 2026; Rules 3, 5–16, 22, 23 with the main group in May 2027. The Act applies to **digital** personal data only. [4][6][8][11]
- **Unsettled:** the Supreme Court issued notice in Feb 2026 on PILs challenging the Act (esp. the s.44(3) amendment to the RTI Act) and referred the matter to a Constitution Bench; no interim stay was granted. Some petitions also target the s.17 exemption clauses (17(1)(c), 17(2)). [9][10]
- **General erasure duty (s.8(7)):** a Data Fiduciary must erase personal data — and cause its processors to erase it — upon consent withdrawal **or** as soon as it is reasonable to assume the specified purpose is no longer served, whichever is earlier, **unless retention is necessary for compliance with any law for the time being in force**. Official illustration: a bank must keep a closed account's KYC for 10 years because banking law requires it — "retention is necessary for compliance with law". [1][6]
- **Inactivity = purpose no longer served (s.8(8) + Rule 8 + Third Schedule):** for listed classes — e-commerce and social-media entities with ≥2 crore registered users, gaming intermediaries with ≥50 lakh — personal data must be erased after **3 years** from the Data Principal's last approach/right-exercise (or Rules commencement, whichever is later), with a **48-hour pre-erasure notice** (Rule 8(2)); exceptions for user-account and virtual-token access; "[…] unless its retention is necessary for compliance with any law for the time being in force". [3][6]
- **Minimum retention floor (Rule 8(3)):** personal data, associated traffic data and processing logs must be kept **at least 1 year** from the date of processing for Seventh Schedule purposes, after which they must be erased "unless further retention is required for compliance with any other law […] or notified by the Government". This is a floor, not a licence to keep indefinitely. [3][6]
- **No fixed general retention period:** the Act sets no universal duration; erasure is purpose- and event-anchored, and the "unless retention is necessary for compliance with any law for the time being in force" carve-out recurs in ss.8(7), 12(3) and Rules 8(1)/(3). [1][3]
- **Correction rights (s.12(1)–(2)):** rights to correction, completion, updating and erasure attach to processing for which consent was given; on request the fiduciary "shall" correct inaccurate/misleading data, complete incomplete data, and update it. No time limit in the section itself. [1][6]
- **Erasure right (s.12(3)):** on a Data Principal's erasure request the fiduciary **shall** erase, "**unless retention […] is necessary for the specified purpose or for compliance with any law for the time being in force**" — note this carve-out is broader than s.8(7)'s, allowing retention needed for the purpose itself. [1][6]
- **Procedure and consequence (Rule 14; PIB):** fiduciaries must publish how to make rights requests and the identifiers needed; grievances must be answered within a period **not exceeding 90 days**. Violations of these obligations are schedule offences carrying penalties up to **₹50 crore** for most fiduciary breaches. [3][5][6][8]
- **Nomination on death or incapacity (s.14):** a Data Principal may nominate an individual who, **on death or incapacity** (unsoundness of mind or infirmity of body), "shall […] exercise the rights of the Data Principal"; nomination follows the fiduciary's terms of service and applicable law (Rule 14(4)). The Act is otherwise silent on deceased persons' data — nomination is the only death hook. [1][3][6]
- **Exemption — legal claims (s.17(1)(a)):** Chapters II (except s.8(1) accountability and s.8(5) security safeguards) and III (rights) and s.16 do **not** apply where processing is "necessary for **enforcing any legal right or claim**". Applies to any fiduciary, not just the State; it can switch off erasure and rights during a claim, but accountability and security duties survive. Whether it extends to *defensive* retention (a litigation hold to resist claims) is not spelled out on the face of the text — treat as unsettled. [1][6]
- **Exemption — courts and regulators (s.17(1)(b)):** processing by courts, tribunals or bodies with judicial/quasi-judicial/regulatory/supervisory functions, where necessary for those functions, is outside Chapters II (bar 8(1), 8(5)) and III — probate/succession court processing sits here. s.17(1)(c) exempts offence prevention/detection/investigation/prosecution. [1][6]
- **Exemptions — State and notified fiduciaries (s.17(3)–(5)):** processing by the State is exempt from s.8(7) erasure and s.12(3) erasure requests outright (conditionally from s.12(2)); Government may notify classes of fiduciaries (including startups) exempt from s.5, s.8(3), s.8(7), s.10 and s.11, and may (within 5 years of commencement) notify any provision as inapplicable to any fiduciary. [1][6]
- **Whole-Act exemptions (s.17(2)):** s.17(2)(b) removes the entire Act for processing necessary for research, archiving or statistical purposes, provided no decision specific to a Data Principal is taken and prescribed standards (Rule 16) are followed. [1][3][6]
- **Other laws prevail in parallel (s.38):** the Act is "in addition to and not in derogation of" other laws in force (already operative), so sectoral record-retention mandates — the 10-year banking illustration, tax, court record rules — continue to operate alongside DPDP rather than being cut down by it. [2][7]

## Implications for LegacyLedger (draft — not decided)

- **Split the permanent audit log from erasable PII.** DPDP treats processing logs containing identifiers as personal data subject to erasure once purpose is served (s.8(7); Rule 8(3) only sets a 1-year floor, not permanence). A "never-deleted" ledger is defensible if it stores non-PII skeleton entries (event type, actor ref, hashes) with PII payloads held under a separate erasable store (e.g. crypto-shredding via key deletion) — the append-only chain then survives erasure without retaining personal data.
- **Frozen post-release estate rows can lean on the "compliance with law" carve-outs and possibly s.17(1)(a).** Retention "necessary for compliance with any law" (s.8(7)/12(3)) is the clean hook for records a succession/probate process may require; processing "necessary for enforcing any legal right or claim" (s.17(1)(a)) may cover the claim-dispute window, but whether it also covers *defensive* legal holds is unsettled on the text — so the design should keep a documented justification path per frozen row, not assume blanket exemption.
- **Append-only corrections look compatible in substance, but verify.** s.12(2) requires inaccurate data be corrected, not necessarily destroyed; a correction-record that supersedes the old value and becomes authoritative plausibly satisfies it while preserving history — but this is a design decision to keep explicit, since a corrigendum could alter the printed Rules and the Board's practice is still emerging.
- **Treat nomination (s.14) as the statutory death/incapacity channel.** LegacyLedger's release trigger maps naturally onto the nominee mechanism; the nominee linkage (and its verification) belongs in the erasable current-record set, whereas the audit of the release event is a candidate for legal-claims retention. Note DPDP is silent on post-mortem data generally — release workflows cannot rely on the Act for anything beyond the nominee's exercise of the deceased's rights.
- **Phasing and uncertainty are planning inputs.** The substantive obligations bite from ~13 May 2027 and face a pending Constitution Bench challenge; a DPDP-compatible storage model (erasable PII over a permanent non-PII audit skeleton) is worth building now both as future-proofing and because it needs no rework if the timeline or the exemption clauses shift.
