# 0031: Claimant Permissions and Shared Documents

- **Status**: Accepted
- **Date**: 2026-10-20
- **Context**:
  Claimants must be able to complete claims for the assets assigned to them without seeing unrelated estate assets. Some source documents cover multiple assets, and an institution may require the complete original document. Access must also preserve a useful read-only record after a claim and estate are closed.

- **Decision**:
  1. While the estate is `ACTIVE`, the owner may assign or remove claimant identities for assets and set access preferences for optional supporting documents. These assignments and preferences lock when a claimant files a release request.
  2. No claimant has estate or asset access before release. On `RELEASE_APPROVED`, the system creates a `permission` grant for each claimant-asset workflow generated by the atomic release transaction.
  3. A grant lets its claimant view masked information for the assigned asset, see its claim checklist and guidance, and work their own claim workflow under that workflow's state rules. It gives no access to other assets or workflows.
  4. A claimant can view and download a complete supporting document when it is linked to an asset assigned to them. If the document is shared across assets, the claimant receives the entire file, including its contents about other assets.
  5. Optional supporting-document access is allowed by default, following ADR 0008. The owner may block a claimant from viewing or downloading an optional, non-required document. A document required by the assigned claim's process rule cannot be blocked; it remains viewable and downloadable. Every claimant document view or download writes an immutable audit event.
  6. A grant stays active while its workflow is active, including during a dispute. Once the workflow is settled or otherwise closed, the grant becomes read-only. Claimants retain read-only access to their own claim history and accessible documents after the estate is archived.
  7. The owner cannot revoke or change a grant after release. The grant rows remain in PostgreSQL's `permission` table and are enforced through authorized read views and stored-procedure actor checks (ADR 0018). A vetoed request never creates claimant grants because the estate was not released.

- **Consequences**:
  - **Positive**: Claimants can complete their assigned claims, including when an institution requires a shared source document, while unrelated asset data stays outside their normal view.
  - **Trade-off**: A shared source file may reveal information about other assets to a claimant, and optional documents blocked by the owner may be unavailable for a claim that does not formally require them.
